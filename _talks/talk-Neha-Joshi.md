Association schemes provide an algebraic framework for studying combinatorial structures through their relations and corresponding adjacency matrices. A central question in this area is determining when collections of relations can be combined to produce new association schemes, a process known as fusion.
In this talk, I will present an overview of my research on fusions of association schemes, beginning with strongly regular graphs and generalized Hamming schemes. I will discuss results from my PhD work, including the role of the Bannai–Muzychuk criterion in identifying and classifying fusions, as well as subsequent work on tensor products and related constructions. I will also highlight connections with more recent developments in the literature, particularly concerning Johnson schemes and amorphic association schemes.
Finally, I will return to some of the questions that motivated my thesis, including conjectures concerning Johnson schemes and perfect matching schemes. I hope to share some of the directions that remain open and encourage further exploration of these beautiful algebraic and combinatorial structures.
